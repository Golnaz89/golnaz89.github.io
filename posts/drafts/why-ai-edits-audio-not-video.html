Why AI Edits Your Audio, Not Your Video

I wanted to build something simple: an app that watches your video and suggests where to cut. Not based on silence detection. Not based on transcripts. Actually looking at the frames and understanding what's happening visually.

Turns out, that's not simple at all. And there's a reason every "AI editing" tool you've heard of—Opus, Descript, SlateAI—is actually analyzing your audio, not your video.

The Math That Killed My App

Let's do the math on analyzing a 30-minute video:

  • 30 minutes × 30 fps = 54,000 frames
  • Even at 1 frame per second = 1,800 images to analyze
  • GPT-4 Vision: ~$0.01-0.03 per image
  • Total: $18-54 per video, minimum

And that's being generous. If you want meaningful visual analysis—understanding composition, detecting when someone looks uncomfortable, identifying the best take—you need more context per frame, which means higher costs.

Now compare that to audio:

  • 30 minutes of audio transcription: ~$0.36 (Whisper)
  • Text analysis of transcript: ~$0.10
  • Total: under $1 per video

That's a 50x cost difference. At minimum.

Why Audio Analysis Actually Works

Tools like Opus and SlateAI aren't cheating—they're being smart. Here's what they can determine from audio alone:

  • Silence detection — obvious cut points
  • Transcript analysis — find the interesting quotes
  • Sentiment analysis — detect enthusiasm, emphasis
  • Filler word detection — cut the "ums" and "ahs"
  • Topic segmentation — break into chapters

For most editing decisions, that's actually enough. The audio tells you what to keep. The video is just... there.

What We're Missing

But there's a whole category of editing decisions that audio can't help with:

  • Visual quality — Is this frame in focus? Is the lighting good?
  • Composition — Is the speaker centered? Is there headroom?
  • Expression — Did they blink? Are they mid-word with a weird face?
  • B-roll timing — When is the speaker gesturing in a way that would hide a cut?
  • Multi-cam selection — Which angle looks best right now?

These are decisions human editors make constantly. And right now, no AI tool is helping with them—because the cost math doesn't work.

When This Will Change

The good news: vision model costs are dropping fast. What cost $0.03 per image last year costs $0.01 now. Give it another two years and we might be at $0.001.

At that point, analyzing every frame of a 30-minute video costs $54 instead of $2,847. Still not cheap, but suddenly viable for professional workflows.

Until then, "AI video editing" really means "AI audio editing with video attached."

And honestly? For most use cases, that's fine. The audio is where the content lives anyway.

But I still want my intelligent cut detector. Someday.
