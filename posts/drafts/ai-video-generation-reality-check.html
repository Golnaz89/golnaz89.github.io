I Spent $50 on AI Video Generation and Got Nothing Usable

I really wanted AI video generation to work. I tried Runway. I tried Pika. I tried Kling. I burned through $50 of my own money experimenting. And I have almost nothing to show for it.

Let me tell you what happened.

The Promise vs. The Reality

The demos are incredible. You've seen them. Stunning cinematic shots generated from text prompts. Camera moves through impossible spaces. Photorealistic humans doing photorealistic things.

The reality? When you actually try to use these tools for real work, everything falls apart.

My Experiment

I had a simple goal: replicate a specific look for a project. Nothing crazy—just a consistent visual style across a few clips that I could cut together.

Here's what happened:

  • Inconsistency. Every generation looked different. Same prompt, different vibe. The lighting changed. The color grade shifted. Forget matching shots.
  • Too short. Most tools max out at a few seconds. When you need actual footage, you need... more than 4 seconds.
  • The glitches. Hands doing impossible things. Faces morphing mid-shot. That unmistakable AI wobble.
  • The cost. Each generation costs credits. Failed generations still cost credits. Iterations cost credits. It adds up fast.

After $50 and hours of prompting, I gave up.

The Flow State Problem

Here's what nobody talks about: creative work requires flow state. You need to get into a rhythm. Ideas build on ideas. You try something, see the result, adjust, iterate.

With AI video generation, you:

  1. Write a prompt
  2. Wait 2-5 minutes
  3. Get something that's not quite right
  4. Try to figure out how to adjust the prompt
  5. Wait again
  6. Repeat

There's no flow. There's no rhythm. It's a slot machine. You're just pulling the lever and hoping.

The Speaker Extension Disaster

Real example: I had a speaker clip that was 3 seconds too short. Just needed to extend the end by a few seconds—the speaker was already still, barely moving. Simple, right?

The AI extension glitched immediately. The face started warping. The background drifted. What should have been invisible became unwatchable.

In the time I spent trying to make it work, I could have just... shot more footage.

The Adobe Parallel

Here's a thought: Adobe won the creative tools race partly because a generation of us learned on pirated copies. Limewire, torrents, whatever—we learned Photoshop and Premiere in college because we could access it. Then we became professionals, and those were the tools we knew.

AI video tools are expensive. There's no "learn on the free tier and pay when you're professional" path. The barrier to experimentation is high. And if you can't experiment freely, you can't build fluency.

I wonder if that matters for adoption.

Where This Might Actually Help

Maybe Hollywood. Maybe the economics make sense when a single shot costs $100K anyway. If AI can get you 80% there and you clean up the rest, maybe that's a win.

But in my world—shipping weekly content, working with real deadlines and real budgets—I haven't found the use case yet. The time I spend wrestling with AI generation is time I could spend actually making things.

I Want To Be Wrong

Seriously. I want this to work. The potential is obvious. But right now, in January 2026, for practical production work? I'm not seeing it.

What's been your experience? Have you found workflows where AI video generation actually delivers? I want to hear the success stories, because I haven't had one yet.
