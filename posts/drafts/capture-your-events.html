If You Had an Event and Didn't Capture It, Did It Even Happen?

I've heard it a hundred times: "We don't want to record the sessions because then people won't register for the event."

I get the fear. I just think it's wrong.

The Fear

Event organizers worry that if they post session recordings, fewer people will show up in person (or register for virtual). Why attend if you can just watch later?

It sounds logical. It's also missing the point of why people go to events in the first place.

Events Aren't Just About Content

Yes, sessions are part of it. But people don't travel to conferences, take time off work, and pay registration fees just to watch talks they could see on YouTube.

They come for:

  • Networking. The hallway conversations. The lunch tables. Meeting people who work on the same problems you do.
  • Hands-on labs. Actually doing the thing with experts in the room who can help when you get stuck.
  • Access. Meeting the speakers. Asking questions. Getting face time with product teams.
  • Energy. Being in a room full of people who care about the same things you care about. That matters.
  • Serendipity. The conversations you didn't plan. The demos you stumbled into. The things you discovered because you were there.

A video of a session doesn't replace any of that.

The Benefits of Capturing Sessions

Extended Reach

Your event had 500 attendees. The recording can reach 50,000. That's not cannibalizing your event—that's multiplying your impact.

Attendee Value

At any multi-track event, attendees have to choose between sessions. Recording means they don't have to miss content they wanted to see. That's a feature, not a bug.

Speaker Visibility

Your speakers put hours into preparing their talks. Giving them a recording they can share extends the life of that work and helps them build their audience.

Evergreen Content

That session on best practices? It's still relevant six months later. A year later. Recordings become a library of value that keeps paying off.

Searchability

With transcripts and chapters, recorded sessions become searchable. Someone Googling a specific problem can find your event, discover your community, and maybe register next year.

The Real Reason People Register

I've run events. I've watched the data. And here's what I've seen: posting recordings doesn't hurt registration. If anything, it helps.

Why? Because the recordings are marketing. People watch a session, realize the content is valuable, and think "I should actually go to this next year."

The content is the proof that the event is worth attending.

A Simple Framework

Think about it this way:

What recordings provide What events provide
Content Content + Context
Information Connection
Watching Participating
Passive Active

They're complements, not substitutes.

Stop Leaving Value on the Table

If you're running an event and not capturing it, you're doing a fraction of what you could be doing. All that effort, all those speakers, all that knowledge—and it evaporates the moment the session ends.

Capture it. Share it. Let it work for you long after the venue is empty.

And if you're worried about registration numbers, focus on making the in-person experience something a video can't replicate. That's the real answer.
