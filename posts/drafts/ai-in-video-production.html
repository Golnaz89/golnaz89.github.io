What AI Has (and Hasn't) Taken Over in Video Production

After 15 years in video production, I've watched a lot of "revolutionary" tools come and go. But AI? This one's actually changing the game. Just not in the ways you might think.

Let me break down what's actually working, what's overhyped, and what I'm still desperately waiting for someone to build.

What AI Has Actually Taken Over

Captions and Transcription

This is the clear winner. Remember when we used to pay per minute for transcription services? Or worse, do it manually? Now auto-generated captions are... actually good. Not perfect, but good enough that cleanup takes minutes instead of hours. For accessibility alone, this has been transformative.

Live Captions

Real-time captioning during live events used to require a professional stenographer. Now we can run live captions that are surprisingly accurate. The latency is still there, but for most use cases, it works.

Voiceover and Text-to-Speech

The quality of AI voices has jumped dramatically. We're using them for placeholder audio, quick explainers, and localization. Are they replacing human voice talent for hero content? Not yet. But for documentation videos and quick turnarounds? Absolutely.

Copyright Detection

Content ID and copyright scanning has gotten scary good. Upload something with even a few seconds of licensed music buried in the background of a conference room? It'll find it. This has made compliance easier and harder at the same time.

Self-Service Publishing

Auto-generated thumbnails, suggested titles, chaptering, highlight clips—platforms are doing a lot of the distribution work that used to be manual. It's not always right, but it's a starting point.

Documentation

AI-generated video summaries, searchable transcripts, auto-generated chapters. Finding the moment in a 2-hour recording where someone said that one thing? Used to be painful. Now it's a search query.

What We're Still Waiting For

Here's my wishlist. The stuff that would actually change my day-to-day if someone solved it:

Intelligent Rough Cuts

I want to feed an AI 4 camera angles and have it make reasonable edit decisions based on who's speaking, reaction shots, and visual interest. We're not there yet.

Consistent B-Roll Generation

More on this in another post, but generating footage that's actually usable and consistent? Still a mess.

Audio Cleanup That Actually Works

Yes, there are tools. No, they don't handle conference room echo, HVAC hum, and someone's laptop fan all at once without making it sound like underwater robots.

Smart Asset Management

I want to say "find me all the clips where Scott Hanselman is explaining something with his hands" and have it work. We're getting closer, but not there.

Automated QC

Check my export for audio sync issues, dropped frames, color space problems, and loudness compliance. All at once. Before I upload. Please.

The Bottom Line

AI hasn't replaced video producers. But it has eliminated a lot of the tedious work that used to eat up our days. The question isn't whether AI will change video production—it already has. The question is what comes next.
